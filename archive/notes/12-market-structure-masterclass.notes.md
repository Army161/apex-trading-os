# 12 - Market Structure Masterclass (Mind Math Money) - Notes

Source: /home/claude/apex/archive/uploads/12-market-structure-masterclass.md (fully read, ~98.5k chars). 14 modules; demo chart is Google (GOOGL) daily, with 1h zoom. Speaker's tool: TradingView.

## 1. Summary
Teaches market structure ("macro": swings, trend, major highs/lows) versus price action ("micro": candles, patterns). Building blocks: swing highs/lows (major vs minor, fractal nature), impulse + pullback as "the basic heartbeat of every trend" (simple vs complex pullbacks), and market states (uptrend, downtrend, range, messy transition = avoid). Introduces multi-timeframe analysis (HTF bias, LTF entry; two timeframes recommended for beginners), Fibonacci retracement (golden zone 0.382-0.618; shallower 0.236-0.382 in strong trends) and measured moves (next impulse ~ equals prior impulse) for targets. Defines mechanical trend-reading signals: Break of Structure (BOS = continuation), Change of Character (CHoCH = first warning; confirmation needs a BOS in the new direction), strong vs weak highs/lows. Two entry models: pullback entry (aggressive at pullback support with hammer/momentum candle, or conservative on breakout of pullback structure with strong candle + volume) and the failure test / 2B / spring / upthrust (false break then close back inside). Covers trend lines/channels (information only), smooth vs choppy regimes (do not trade chop), order flow basics (volume, fixed-range volume profile, footprint, delta, VWAP), market participants (speculators, arbitrageurs, hedgers), and ends with a 10-step workflow.

## 2. Codifiable rules
- Uptrend = HH + HL: "in order to have an uptrend, the definition of an uptrend is that we need to have both consecutive higher highs and consecutive higher lows. So when we have two higher highs and two higher lows, we can consider an uptrend to have been started." Downtrend = LH + LL. Range: "lows and highs stay at around the same level" (do not need to be exact).
- Swing high: "a peak where the price turned downward"; swing low: "a valley where the price turned upward". Mark major swings first; minor swings are inside each move (fractal). (Exact N-bar pivot lookback NOT given: "you will learn mechanical rules ... later" but none stated; implement as configurable pivot length, e.g. 3-5 bars, and flag as assumption.)
- Market state filter: "different market states requires different trading strategies." "never force a trend trading strategy in a trading range or never force a trading range strategy if the market is trending." Transition/messy state: "try to avoid it completely."
- Impulse move: "bigger candles ... more candles of the same color ... clear momentum"; in uptrend large green candles, smaller red ones. Pullback: "mixed candles ... smaller bodies ... more wicks", dojis, "not as steep". Preferred volume: rising on impulses, lower on pullbacks ("lots of volume on a pullback and especially if the pullback is more sharp, that might be a slight warning sign").
- Simple pullback = one clean counter move; complex pullback = multi-leg. "for beginners, I actually highly recommend to start with the simple pullback".
- BOS: "when the price breaks the previous swing point in the trend direction" (uptrend: breaks swing high). "as long as the price is holding the low of the trend ... we are still in an uptrend."
- CHoCH: "the first meaningful break against the established trend" (uptrend: price breaks below the last low / the higher low); can mean change to downtrend OR to sideways. "a change of character is no guarantee that the trend is changing. In order to actually confirm a complete trend change, we need a break of structure after the change of character."
- MSS (market structure shift) treated as synonym of CHoCH; speaker says to ignore the term.
- Strong level: "a level that caused a successful break of structure" (the origin of the move that broke structure, e.g. the low where the impulse that made BOS started = strong low). Weak level: "a level that failed to break structure or ... failed to cause a change of character" (e.g. a high whose down move did not break the prior strong low = weak high; it becomes strong if the down move later causes a CHoCH). Strong low "tends to hold": use for stops and bounce entries; weak levels "often get taken out".
- Multi-timeframe: HTF = bias/context (daily or 4h; day traders 1h or 30m); middle = structure/levels; lowest = precise entry. "for most beginners ... two time frames is definitely enough." "when all three time frames align your probability of success increases dramatically."
- Fibonacci retracement: draw swing low -> swing high in an uptrend. Levels named: 0, 0.236, 0.382, 0.618 (also 0.5 implied by the golden zone). "the golden zone is the zone here between the 38% to 62%"; "in very strong trends, the pullbacks tend to end in ... 0.236 to 0.382". "Never ... only use Fibonacci." TradingView hotkey Alt+F.
- Measured move: "the next impulse often equals the size of the previous one"; copy impulse length from the pullback low to set target.
- Pullback entry A: need uptrend (HH, HL, BOS). Draw resistance and support lines of the pullback. Aggressive: enter at the support of the pullback on a bullish signal (hammer or "strong momentum candle"); entry at candle close of the hammer; stop "just below the hammer". Conservative: wait for the breakout of the pullback structure with "a strong bullish candle ... momentum candle" and "lots of volume"; stop "below the low of the pullback"; target = measured move (impulse length from pullback low) or fixed R:R. Fixed R:R examples: 1:1, 2:1, 3:1; 1.5R used in the flag example.
- Failure test (2B / Wyckoff spring / upthrust): "when the price breaks a level briefly and then snaps back ... the entry is actually when the price recloses inside the range." Entry at the candle close; stop "past the wick of the failure" ("above or below the extreme of the pattern"); target fixed R:R (e.g. 2:1). Works with trend (spring at pullback support) or counter-trend (failed BOS in uptrend then short). Context matters (long strong trend before).
- Bull-flag entry on LTF: draw downward-sloping channel on the 1h pullback; entry = strong green candle closing above the upper (falling) trend line; stop just below the low of the pattern; target fixed 1.5R.
- Trend lines: rising line connects higher lows, falling connects lower highs. A trend-line break is information only: "not necessarily that ... we're automatically shifting from an uptrend to a downtrend ... I actually strongly recommend against [trading purely on trend lines]." Channel = parallel line at the top; price reaching channel top is a likely reversal/target area; breaking above channel => steeper trend.
- Regimes: smooth = long clear impulses, clean pullbacks, clear BOS/CHoCH (trade); choppy = overlapping swings, many fake signals, stops hit constantly (avoid; "simply waiting is often the most ... logical and best option"). Lower timeframes (1m) are more choppy than daily.
- Volume: use basic "Volume" indicator; "the more stocks that get traded during a specific candle ... the stronger and more significant the move becomes." Breakaway gap + volume spike marked start of the Google trend. Fixed-range volume profile: high-volume price node acts like a "magnet" (slight rule of thumb). Also mentioned: footprint, delta, VWAP (no settings).
- 10-step workflow: (1) identify market state; (2) mark major swing highs/lows; (3) note BOS and CHoCH; (4) identify strong vs weak levels; (5) evaluate pullback quality; (6) choose entry model (pullback or failure test); (7) refine with lower timeframe; (8) optional indicators (at least volume); (9) set stop-loss; (10) targets via measured move or fixed R:R. (Speaker lists 8-10; stop and target are the final steps.)
- Stop placement options in the demo: below the low of the whole structure, or below the nearest higher-low inside the pullback if the breakout follows a smaller swing.

## 4. Risk and psychology rules
- No numeric risk % in this course. "Always set stop-loss," stop placement rules above; define stop and target before entry.
- "Trading is much more about waiting. You wait and wait and wait for the perfect conditions and that is when you trade." Do not force trades in chop; "reduce size" less important than simply waiting in choppy markets.
- Never rely on one concept (e.g., Fibonacci) alone; "you will probably most likely lose a lot of money."
- Avoid blaming manipulation: "the only way to improve as a trader is to accept your losses, analyze and back test and forward test your strategies and improve them over time."
- Back test then forward test before trading with lots of money; refine and drop unneeded steps with testing.
- Can never be 100% sure: "our goal is to flip the odds to be in our favor."
