## 2. Codifiable rules
- Indicators: "mathematical formulas that are based on price and volume that makes the charts easier to read." "indicators do not predict the future."
- RSI: created 1978 by J. Welles Wilder; "measures momentum"; scale 0-100; default length 14 ("the standard setting is that the length here is 14"); standard levels 70/30. "when the RSI is above 70 ... overbought ... many traders think that ... you should sell ... and this is completely wrong." Strong overbought (>80) = strong momentum. Calm market: RSI near 50.
- RSI fast settings (lower timeframes e.g. 15m): "change this value to around 7 to 10" (example: 7), upper band 80, lower band 20, remove RSI-based moving average (default MA length 14, uncheck) and background fill. Do not enable the built-in "calculate divergences" setting blindly: "you will get many false signals."
- RSI divergence: bearish = price higher high while RSI lower high -> reversal down; bullish = price lower low while RSI higher low -> reversal up. Strongest when RSI is beyond extreme levels: "divergences when we see ... the RSI either dipping below 20 or above 80." Equal-high price with lower RSI high is a weaker form of bearish divergence ("the signal is stronger when we have higher highs on the price and lower highs on the indicator").
- MACD: developed by Gerald Appel (late 1970s); components MACD line, signal line, histogram. Classic: MACD crossing above signal = bullish momentum; speaker says this is "way too simplistic".
- MACD "Linda Raschke settings" ("31016"): fast length 3, slow length 10, source close (or OHLC4 "not huge" difference), signal smoothing 16, oscillator MA type EMA, signal line MA type SMA. "These settings right here are known as the Linda Raschke settings."
- MACD trade: "looking out for a strong momentum move. To find these moves, we want the MACD line ... to be much higher than the previous peaks." Then wait for pullback, "buy this pullback and catch the next move to the upside". Entry methods: chart patterns (bull flag) or candlestick patterns; simplest: "enter at the breakout" of the flag (or at flag support / failure test). Stop "below the low of the flag"; target "risk-to-reward ratio of two". (Mirror for shorts implied.)
- Fibonacci: key ratios 0.382, 0.618, 0.5 ("not a Fibonacci number" but pullbacks average about half the impulse); also shows 0.236, 0.786. Uptrend: draw swing low -> swing high; downtrend: swing high -> swing low. Golden zone broad = 38.2%-61.8% (stricter 50%-61.8%). Customize to show only 0, 0.382, 0.618 and shade between 0.382 and 0.618. Define trend first (HH+HL). Not enough alone: combine with S/R flip, candlestick pattern (example: golden zone + broken-resistance-turned-support + bullish engulfing = 3 bullish factors, "often enough for us to take a trade").
- ATR: created by J. Welles Wilder 1978; measures volatility (average price movement per candle). Default length 14, smoothing RMA ("the original calculation method ... I do recommend"). Keep consistent with RSI length. Short-term traders (5m/15m): length 7-10, optionally EMA smoothing. Swing/long-term: 20-50. Read value at entry candle.
- ATR trade rules: "set the stop loss around 2x the ATR" from entry; "a common profit target multiple is 4x" = fixed risk-to-reward of 2. Example: ATR 3,400 -> stop 6,800 below entry (just below golden zone); can add "wiggle room" below. Other uses: position sizing, trailing stop, breakout confirmation.
- Pivot Points High Low: settings pivot high 20 / pivot low 20 left/right length ("2020") to keep only significant pivots. Use clusters of pivots as S/R zones (draw using real bodies OR wicks, "the most important part is that you are consistent"). Pattern: higher highs/lows into resistance (ascending triangle); breakout is "a solid entry level". Entry at candle close of breakout; stop 2x ATR (example ATR 40 -> stop 80); target 2R.
- Auto Candlestick Patterns (TradingView built-in, search "all candlestick patterns"): bullish blue, bearish red by default. Recommended patterns: bullish engulfing, hammer, shooting star, inverted hammer, hanging man (also bearish engulfing implied). "detect trend based on" default SMA 50 ("will filter out many false signals"); "no detection" produces many false signals. Only trade pattern at key S/R ("these patterns become so much stronger because they appear at a key support level"). Hammer stop: "immediately below the pattern" (or below the support area if that is nearby).
- Auto Chart Patterns (needs Premium): detects triangles, wedges, head & shoulders, flags, double tops/bottoms; shows breakout areas and targets; uses pivot points internally. Settings: show only chosen patterns (e.g. double top/bottom or bull/bear flags), show price targets.
- Confluence rule: "The more factors we combine ... the more we tilt the odds in our favor." Example short on US100 double top: RSI bearish divergence + bearish chart pattern + bearish engulfing on neckline break; stop 2x ATR (ATR ~425 -> stop ~850), target 2R.
- Win-rate math: with 2:1 reward, "we don't even have to win 40% of the time to still make money" (breakeven 33.3%).

## 4. Risk and psychology rules
- No risk % or drawdown rules. "not all trades will succeed" mindset; 2R targets mean breakeven win rate is about 33%.
- Position sizing via ATR is mentioned but not detailed.
- Do not rely on a single indicator; require confluence; keep charts clean ("indicators ... to make the chart easier to read, not harder").
- Learn to spot patterns manually first because "indicators ... are not perfect and sometimes ... make false signals".
